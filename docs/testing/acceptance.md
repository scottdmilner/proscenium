# Acceptance Criteria

These criteria define what passing means. **Correctness is established mainly by small bespoke fixtures**, each built to exercise specific requirements. Production-scale scenes such as ALab confirm that everything holds together and performs at scale; they are not the main source of correctness evidence. How tests are organized is in [strategy.md](strategy.md).

## Bespoke Fixtures

- The requirement-to-test matrix covers every requirement applicable to Phases 1 and 2 in PROJECT.md, spec/, and testing/, with high-risk entries marked separately by M4 from its risk matrix. Every requirement is covered by at least one bespoke fixture with a defined test or oracle and qualifying milestone. Missing requirement rows or missing coverage block phase qualification. A production-scale run is never the only evidence for a requirement.
- Requirements that are not runtime behavior are covered without a fixture: a contract the spec says a milestone must settle is covered by that milestone's decision record, and a development or test-process rule by a tooling or CI check. The behavior a settled contract defines gets its own rows and fixtures. Permissions, scope limits, and design guidance with no checkable outcome are listed in the matrix with the milestone they guide, and need no coverage.
- Fixtures are small, generated by code, and deterministic. Each targets one behavior, or a deliberate combination such as `F-NESTED-STRESS`.
- Expected results come from the spec, not from recording the implementation's output.
- Each fixture case declares its operation and expected outcome: synchronization that completes successfully or with issues, expected synchronization failure, or a lifecycle operation. Its cases and assertions also identify the applicable requirement IDs and qualification phase from the requirement-to-test matrix. A fixture may contain cases with different outcomes or phases; each case uses the applicable checks below. M12 qualifies only Phase 1 cases and assertions. M13 qualifies Phase 2 cases and assertions and reruns Phase 1 coverage as regression checks.
- Fixtures with time-varying content are evaluated at the [sampled time codes](#sampled-time-codes). Fixtures with payloads run under both payload policies.

### Standard End-to-End Checks

Applied to every end-to-end synchronization case expected to complete successfully or with issues, bespoke or production-scale, at each sampled time code and payload policy that case uses. Expected failures and lifecycle operations use their own checks below:

- Synchronization completes without a fatal failure.
- Every supported mesh in the fixture's expected inventory is accounted for in source/display correspondence, and its viewport and render participation match evaluated visibility and configured purpose inclusion.
- Deferred content types appear as placeholders or markers with aggregated diagnostics.
- World bounds of each model's display match USD-computed bounds under the bounds comparison contract.
- Geometry follows the [Geometry Sharing](../spec/support/instancing.md#geometry-sharing) contract: equivalent uses share resources, required distinct geometry or appearance data remains correct, and equivalent variants remain deduplicated. Each distinct-resource case has a recorded display reason and correctness oracle. Separate bindings do not share or contaminate managed resources. Unique mesh-data counts are recorded as an aggregate regression metric; lower counts do not excuse incorrect display.
- An unchanged second synchronization keeps active display resource counts and source/display diagnostics stable under [Resource Stability](../spec/lifecycle.md#resource-stability). When disposal succeeds, total managed-resource counts also remain stable. Count inactive pending-cleanup resources separately; disposal failures may increase only those tracked counts and their current cleanup diagnostics.
- Save and reopen preserves the display and correspondence without accessing the source.
- Changing the time code or payload policy and resynchronizing gives the same result as a clean synchronization at the new settings.
- Each sampled time code cached in a generation synchronized at another displays what a clean synchronization at it does, whatever the order of caching; a frame between cached time codes displays the latest one before it.

### Expected-Failure Checks

For synchronization cases expected to fail before publication, including an unavailable source and injected preparation or publication failures:

- The attempt reports fatal failure with the expected categorized diagnostics and attempted settings recorded in the last-attempt state.
- The entire previous published generation remains unchanged: its settings, geometry, materials, images, flags, ownership, correspondence, and selection-resolution state. On first synchronization, no active partial display appears.
- Prepared resources are disposed of or remain inactive and persistently tracked as pending cleanup with ownership evidence and diagnostics. No untracked partial content remains, under the [Synchronization Transaction](../spec/pipeline.md#synchronization-transaction) contract.
- No synchronization undo step is added, including when global undo is enabled. Binding configuration and the published generation remain unchanged; the last-attempt record and pending-cleanup tracking may change.
- If disposal also fails, save/reopen preserves pending work, and explicit retry after removing the failure resolves it without USD access. Retries preserve active, detached, reused, and externally required resources.

Retirement failures after publication are completed-with-issues cases, not failed synchronization cases. Apply the standard end-to-end checks with their separate accounting for active and pending-cleanup resources, and verify that the new generation remains active and obsolete leftovers stay tracked, noncanonical, excluded from the active display and correspondence, and reported as retryable pending cleanup.

For disposal-failure cases before or after publication, repeat attempts with disposal still failing. Every retained additional resource must be inactive and persistently accounted for as pending cleanup; the active display must not accumulate duplicate resources. Source/display diagnostics remain equivalent for unchanged evaluated content, while current cleanup diagnostics may change with unresolved work and remain deduplicated by operation and resource. After removing the failures, explicit cleanup retry must restore managed-resource counts to the clean baseline for the preserved or published generation and remove resolved cleanup diagnostics, under Resource Stability. Account separately for resources safely released intact to protected users rather than deleting them to satisfy a count.

### Lifecycle Checks

Cleanup, detach, and binding removal cases assert their operation-specific requirements in [Ownership and Lifecycle](../spec/lifecycle.md#ownership-and-lifecycle), [Lifecycle Failure Safety](../spec/lifecycle.md#lifecycle-failure-safety), and [Undo and Runtime State](../spec/lifecycle.md#undo-and-runtime-state). Successful-synchronization inventory and bounds checks do not apply to the lifecycle operation itself; any synchronization cases in the same fixture still use the applicable synchronization checks.

- Detach preserves surviving ordinary Blender data as-is, including edits and damage, and removes only binding-specific metadata and controls. It requires no USD access or reconstruction; permitted appearance changes are reported. Later refresh and cleanup preserve the detached result.
- Failure injection verifies before-commit preservation, consistent ownership and correspondence after committed partial progress, accurate outcomes and pending-work diagnostics, and safe retry after save/reopen without USD access.
- Binding removal never orphans unresolved disposal work or harms detached or unrelated content. Undo/redo restores content, configuration, ownership, and pending-cleanup tracking together; with global undo enabled outside background mode, only invocations that commit lifecycle changes add an undo step.

### Interior Correctness

Matching model bounds do not establish correctness inside a model. Targeted source-to-display checks and small semantic fixtures cover internal meshes, transforms within models, material assignments, UV selection, face winding, and per-instance appearance. Material fixtures use headless render assertions on controlled scenes, without requiring equivalence with USD renderers.

## Sampled Time Codes

Synchronization and caching must work **at any time code**. That remains a behavioral guarantee; the sampled set is evidence, not exhaustive proof. For each time-varying fixture it covers at least:

- The stage's start and end time codes.
- Interior samples, including a non-integer time code.
- Selected authored sample times.
- Samples around topology or visibility transitions, and midpoints between relevant samples.
- Time codes before the start and after the end.
- The USD default time code.
- Deterministically seeded additional samples where practical.

Each time-varying fixture declares its topology and visibility transitions from how it is built, and seeds its additional samples from its fixture ID. Non-integer offsets are binary fractions, so every sampled time code is exactly representable. A fixture without time-varying content is evaluated at the default time code unless a case names another.

A bespoke fixture's selected authored sample times are all of them, or 8 spread evenly when it has more, and it has 2 seeded samples. A production-scale fixture has no authored or seeded samples beyond the other kinds listed above, since each sample costs a synchronization at scale.

## Bounds Comparison Contract

The bounds check reuses decisions made elsewhere rather than making its own. It compares each destination (viewport and render) separately, with visibility and purpose as evaluated under [Visibility and Purpose](../spec/support/visibility.md#visibility-and-purpose), in Blender world space after [Coordinate Conversion](../spec/support/transforms.md#coordinate-conversion).

M4 defines in this section, for the chosen representation: whether expected bounds come from evaluated geometry or authored extent hints; which geometry state is compared where the display approximates or extends USD geometry, such as subdivision and displacement; how bounding-box placeholders are compared; absolute and relative tolerances; and empty or unknown bounds. Deferred content without usable bounds is excluded rather than compared against empty markers.

For the display representation M4 chose (objects, with batches of at least 5):

- **Models:** each prim whose kind is a model kind (component, group, assembly, or one derived from them) is compared, once per drawn copy, inside native instances too. Its display bounds in a destination are the axis-aligned box of the world-space positions of every vertex displayed there for it or its descendants, as correspondence assigns them: an object's mesh under the object's world matrix, and a batch's instance's prototype mesh under the instance's transform and its carriers' world matrix.
- **Expected bounds come from evaluated geometry:** each displayed prim's points at the time code, under USD's local-to-world transform and the coordinate conversion, boxed after they are transformed. Authored extent hints are not used: `UsdGeom.BBoxCache` transforms them as a box, which is looser under rotation (a triangle rotated 45° gives a y extent of 1.414 against its points' 0.707, USD 0.26.3), and an authored extent may be stale.
- **Geometry state:** a subdivision surface is compared as its control cage on both sides, as USD's points are, and the display's refinement of it isn't. Displacement through a material changes no mesh positions and isn't compared.
- **Approximations:** a prim displayed as a diagnosed approximation of its geometry, such as an implicit shape's mesh or a NURBS patch's control hull, lies within its USD-computed extent under its world transform and spans at least 90% of it on every axis. A model holding approximations has display bounds that contain the box of its exactly displayed content and lie within that box joined with its approximations' transformed extents.
- **Placeholders:** a bounding-box placeholder's eight corners are its usable bounds' corners under its world transform, compared corner by corner: its authored extent, used as given since it is what the placeholder shows, or else USD's computed extent for curves and points. The model's expected bounds include those corners.
- **Empty and unknown bounds:** empty markers and content without usable bounds are excluded from both sides. A model with no compared content in a destination expects no displayed vertices there. A model whose expected bounds aren't finite, from nonfinite positions or transforms, isn't compared; its [classification](../spec/support/geometry.md#mesh-classification) is checked instead.
- **Tolerance:** each face of a display box is within 1e-6 + 1e-5 × m of the expected box's in Blender world units, where m is the largest absolute coordinate of the model's expected box; Blender holds positions and matrices as 32-bit floats, and this is about a hundred of their units in the last place at that magnitude. A placeholder's corners use the same tolerance.

## Production-Scale Validation: ALab

ALab checks that the synchronizer works and performs on a real production-scale scene, catching integration and scale problems that small fixtures miss. A failure found in ALab is reproduced as a bespoke fixture before it is fixed.

- ALab **v2.3.0** with techvar assets **v2.2.0**, opened through the primary `entry.usda` file.
- The tree is pinned by [alab/subtrees.sha256](alab/subtrees.sha256) and checked with `tools/alab_checksums.py --check`. [alab/README.md](alab/README.md) describes how it is installed and what the inventory shows.
- ALab passes the [standard end-to-end checks](#standard-end-to-end-checks) at every sampled time code and under every payload policy (load-all and load-no-payload).
- Run an inventory script for each payload policy that counts prim types, applied schemas, and shaders, including content inside native-instance prototypes together with its instance counts. Deferred content in ALab is then known in advance, and the checks are judged against the content that policy actually loads.

## Performance

- The reference hardware is an **M1 Max MacBook Pro**. The [feasibility record](../feasibility/runtime-record.md#environment) records its memory configuration, OS version, and Blender build.
- Measurements run in the Blender binary on AC power, with no other heavy load, and record the OS version and Blender build hash. A change to either starts a new baseline.
- Record repeated-run statistics, not single timings.
- Measure separately: cold and warm source acquisition, evaluation, planning, transfer across any compiled-core boundary, Blender resource construction, publication and retirement, undo overhead, peak memory while old and new generations coexist, inspection-tree expansion, source-to-display lookup, and selection and highlight updates.
- Measure these scenarios: initial synchronization, unchanged manual refresh, single transform edit, shared geometry edit affecting many instances, single material parameter change, payload-policy switch, and topology-changing time step.
- ALab provides production-scale numbers. Generated fixtures of controlled size show how each cost scales.
- M4 sets provisional budgets for comparing representations. Phase 1 records baselines on the reference hardware, not gates. Phase 2 targets are derived from those baselines (for example, "a single transform edit in ALab updates within a set time").
- Performance claims cover synchronization and interaction-function latency, not overall viewport responsiveness.

## Where It Runs

- Bespoke fixtures run in ordinary CI.
- A smaller generated fixture with production-like structure (nested native instancing, multi-material assets, payloads, and deep organizational hierarchy) runs in ordinary CI as an integration check.
- ALab runs as a manual or nightly job, not in ordinary CI.
